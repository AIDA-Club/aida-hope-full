# Early detection and screening

Always end screening answers with: **"Ask your doctor what's right for you."**

## Two parts of early detection (WHO)
- **Early diagnosis:** knowing the signs and getting checked promptly when something changes, at any age.
- **Screening:** checking healthy people with **no symptoms**, mainly with mammograms.
- If you have a symptom, you need a check-up now, not a screening appointment.

## UAE guidance (applies in Ajman and Sharjah)
| Authority | Mammogram | Other advice |
|---|---|---|
| MoHAP National Guideline (2014) | Every 2 years, ages 40–69 | Yearly clinical breast exam at 40–69. Ages 20–39: breast awareness. Women 20+ should learn breast self-exam. |
| EHS (runs public facilities in Ajman, Sharjah and the northern emirates) | "Every two years starting from age 40" | Monthly self-exam from age 20. Under 40 with a family history: "annual screenings are advised." |
| ADPHC Abu Dhabi | Every 2 years from 40 | Monthly self-exam from 20. Clinical exam every 3 years from 20 and yearly from 40. Higher-risk women may screen more often. |
| DHA Dubai Periodic Screening Guideline (2024) | Every 2 years for all women over 40 | Earlier start for family history or a known gene change, as the doctor advises. |

- **Bottom line in the UAE: a mammogram every 2 years from age 40.**
- MoHAP 2014: screening mammograms are "not recommended before age of 30," and under 40 only if at increased risk.
- Free screening is mainly offered through time-limited campaigns (for example Pink Caravan in October), not as a standing entitlement for everyone. See the help file.

## Self-exam and breast self-awareness
- **UAE (EHS, ADPHC):** a monthly self-exam from age 20, to get to know your normal.
  - EHS timing: at the end of each period, or the start of each month after menopause.
  - ADPHC timing: about 6–10 days after your period starts.
- **International (WHO, ACS, NHS):** focus on "breast awareness." Know how your breasts normally look and feel, and report changes. Studies did not show that formal self-exam lowers deaths (IARC 2015). ACS says self-exams are "not sufficient for cancer screening."
- **How to combine them:** do the monthly self-check the UAE recommends, keep up mammograms from 40, and see a doctor promptly about any change.

## Higher-risk groups (start earlier, ask a doctor)
MoHAP 2014 higher-risk examples:
- Chest radiation before age 30
- Previous breast cancer
- LCIS or atypical cells found on a biopsy
- Strong family history, or a BRCA1/2 (or TP53/PTEN) gene change
- First-degree relative of a BRCA carrier

What may be offered:
- Earlier or yearly mammograms.
- Yearly MRI "as indicated."
- Ultrasound as an extra test for dense breasts.
- Genetic counselling (for example, a relative diagnosed at 45 or younger, or male breast cancer in the family).

Exact ages differ between bodies; never give personal schedules.

## International comparison
| Body | Average-risk advice |
|---|---|
| WHO | Organised mammography "usually women 50-69" |
| IARC (2015) | Clear benefit at 50–69, also 70–74; limited evidence at 40–49 |
| USPSTF, US (2024) | Every 2 years, 40–74 |
| American Cancer Society | Optional yearly 40–44; yearly 45–54; every 1–2 years from 55 |
| American College of Radiology (2023) | Risk assessment by age 25 to see if screening before 40 is needed |
| NHS England | Every 3 years, 50 up to the 71st birthday |
| European guidelines (2019) | Not at 40–44; every 2–3 years at 45–49; every 2 years at 50–69; every 3 years at 70–74 |

- Bodies weigh benefits (fewer deaths) against harms (false alarms, extra tests) differently. Follow UAE guidance locally.

## Men
- No routine screening for men (ACS).
- Men with a strong family history or a BRCA change may benefit from careful breast exams.
- Any lump or nipple change in a man needs a doctor's check.

## Under 40 and university students
- No routine screening mammogram under 40 unless at higher risk (MoHAP).
- Know your normal, follow the UAE monthly self-check advice, and see a doctor about any change.
- If you find a lump:
  - Under 30: ultrasound is usually the first imaging test.
  - Ages 30–39: ultrasound and/or mammogram.
  - 40 and over: diagnostic mammogram (ACR).
- With a family history, ask a doctor whether earlier checks are needed (EHS, DHA).

## What to expect
**Mammogram (about 30 minutes):**
- Do not wear deodorant, powder or lotion under the arms or on the breasts. They can look like spots on the image.
- The best time is about a week after your period. Avoid the week before it if your breasts are tender then.
- Tell staff about symptoms, past surgery, hormone use, family history or possible pregnancy.
- Each breast is pressed between plates for a few seconds. This can be uncomfortable; tell the technologist if it hurts.
- The dose is very low, about 0.4 mSv (ACS).
- A radiologist reads the images. Ask when results will come; do not assume no news is good news.
- About 5–15% of screening mammograms need more tests, and most of these turn out normal.
- Mammograms miss about 1 in 8 cancers, so new changes still need checking.

**Breast ultrasound:**
- Uses gel and a handheld probe, with sound waves and **no radiation**.
- It should not be painful.
- Used to check lumps, tell cysts from solid lumps, add detail for dense breasts, and guide biopsies.
- It does not replace mammograms.
