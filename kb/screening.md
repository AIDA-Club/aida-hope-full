# Early detection: early diagnosis and screening

Source: WHO breast cancer fact sheet (checked 9 October 2026).

Early detection has two parts:

1. **Early diagnosis**: knowing the signs of breast cancer and seeing a doctor
   promptly if you notice a change. This matters at any age.
2. **Screening**: checking healthy women who have no symptoms, to find cancer
   before symptoms appear. For breast cancer this usually means mammography
   in women aged about 50–69.

Why it matters: cancers found early, while they are small and have not spread,
are more likely to be treated successfully.

What is right for one person depends on their age, history and health. Ask
your doctor what's right for you. This booth cannot recommend a screening
schedule for anyone.
