# Medical terms in plain words

**Benign**: not cancer.

**Malignant**: cancer. Malignant cells can grow into nearby tissue and spread.

**Biopsy**: taking a small sample of tissue so it can be examined. A biopsy is
how doctors confirm whether a lump is benign or malignant.

**Mammogram (mammography)**: a breast imaging test. Mammography is the test
used to screen healthy women, usually aged 50–69, for breast cancer.

**Ultrasound**: a scan that uses sound waves, not radiation, to make a picture
of the inside of the breast. It is often used alongside a mammogram to look
at a breast lump. The images in our booth challenge are breast ultrasound
images.

**Lump**: an area that feels different from the tissue around it. Most breast
lumps are not cancer, but any unusual lump should be checked by a doctor.

**Screening**: testing healthy people with no symptoms to find cancer early.
For breast cancer this usually means mammography in women aged about 50–69.

**Early diagnosis**: knowing the signs of breast cancer and seeing a doctor
promptly when something changes.

**Metastasis**: when cancer cells spread from where they started to other
parts of the body.

**In situ**: "in its original place". A cancer described as "in situ" has not
grown into the surrounding tissue.

**BI-RADS**: a standard system that radiologists use to describe breast
imaging results. It sorts what they see into categories that show how
suspicious a finding looks and suggest what should happen next, such as
routine screening, a follow-up scan or a biopsy. Only a doctor can explain
what a category means for a particular person.
