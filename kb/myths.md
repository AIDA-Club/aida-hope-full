# Breast cancer myths and facts

Verdicts: MYTH, FACT, PARTLY TRUE, NOT ENOUGH EVIDENCE. Answer kindly; never mock a belief.

## Causes and risk

**Myth: Deodorants or antiperspirants cause breast cancer.**
Verdict: MYTH. The best studies found no link between underarm products and breast cancer (NCI, ACS). The rumour reportedly began as an email hoax.

**Myth: Underwire or tight bras, or sleeping in a bra, cause breast cancer.**
Verdict: MYTH. "There is no scientific or clinical data that shows that wearing a bra changes the risk" (ACS). A badly fitting bra can be uncomfortable, but it does not cause cancer.

**Myth: A hit or injury to the breast causes cancer.**
Verdict: MYTH. Injuries do not cause breast cancer. They can cause harmless lumps or make you notice an existing lump, so a lump that does not go away should still be checked.

**Myth: Bigger breasts mean higher risk.**
Verdict: MYTH. Breast size is not linked to risk. Larger breasts can make changes harder to feel. (Extra body weight after menopause is a risk factor, which is different.)

**Myth: Breast implants cause breast cancer.**
Verdict: MYTH. Implants have not been shown to raise breast cancer risk (ACS). They are linked to a rare lymphoma, BIA-ALCL, which "is not breast cancer" (FDA). Changes near an implant should be shown to a surgeon.

**Myth: A mobile phone in your bra causes breast cancer.**
Verdict: MYTH. Phone energy does not damage genes, and the best studies do not show phones cause cancer (NCI). Little research has looked at phones in bras specifically.

**Myth: Abortion or miscarriage causes breast cancer.**
Verdict: MYTH. Expert reviews found that neither raises later breast cancer risk (NCI, ACS).

**Myth: Birth control pills cause breast cancer.**
Verdict: PARTLY TRUE. Hormonal pills are linked to a small rise in risk while taking them, which fades after stopping. They also lower the risk of ovarian, womb and bowel cancers (NCI). Discuss choices with a doctor.

**Myth: Hair dye causes breast cancer.**
Verdict: NOT ENOUGH EVIDENCE. Results are "mixed and inconsistent" (NCI); there is no firm conclusion.

**Myth: Stress causes breast cancer, or a positive attitude cures it.**
Verdict: NOT ENOUGH EVIDENCE for stress; MYTH for attitude. Whether stress is linked to cancer "is not clear," and a UK study of over 100,000 women found no link with breast cancer. There is no convincing evidence that attitude affects risk or survival, so no one should be blamed for their feelings (NCI).

**Myth: Breast cancer is contagious.**
Verdict: MYTH. You cannot catch it or pass it on (NCI). Visiting, hugging and caring for a patient is safe.

## Food and drink

**Myth: Sugar "feeds" or causes breast cancer.**
Verdict: MYTH. Sugar does not directly cause or feed it (NCI). Too many calories can cause weight gain, and extra weight after menopause raises risk.

**Myth: Dairy or soy foods cause breast cancer.**
Verdict: MYTH. A World Cancer Research Fund review found no link with dairy (via CRUK). Soy foods show no increased risk, even for survivors (AICR); concentrated soy supplements are a different matter.

**Myth: Coffee causes breast cancer.**
Verdict: NOT ENOUGH EVIDENCE of harm. A large US study of over 77,000 older women found no link between coffee and breast cancer.

**Myth: Breastfeeding makes no difference.**
Verdict: MYTH. Breastfeeding slightly lowers risk: about 4.3% lower for every 12 months (Lancet 2002). Not everyone can breastfeed, and no one should be blamed.

## Who gets it

**Myth: Men cannot get breast cancer.**
Verdict: MYTH. About 0.5–1% of breast cancers occur in men (WHO). Men should get any lump or nipple change checked. In an Ajman survey, 38% of female students believed men cannot get it.

**Myth: I'm too young to worry.**
Verdict: MYTH. Risk rises with age, but about 10% of US cases are in women under 45 (CDC). In the UAE, diagnosis is often 10–15 years younger than in Europe or North America.

**Myth: No family history means no risk.**
Verdict: MYTH. Most people diagnosed have no family history; inherited genes cause only a small share of cases (WHO, ACS).

**Myth: A family history or a BRCA gene means I will definitely get it.**
Verdict: MYTH. These raise risk, sometimes a lot, but do not make cancer certain. A doctor or genetic counsellor can explain options.

## Lumps, tests and treatment

**Myth: A painless lump is nothing to worry about.**
Verdict: MYTH. Lumps are "often without pain" (WHO). Any new lump should be checked.

**Myth: Every lump means cancer.**
Verdict: MYTH. Most breast lumps are not cancer; many are cysts or fibroadenomas (NHS). Only a doctor's check can tell.

**Myth: Breast pain means cancer.**
Verdict: MYTH. Pain on its own is not usually a sign (NHS). Pain that is new, persistent or comes with other changes should still be checked.

**Myth: Mammogram radiation causes cancer.**
Verdict: MYTH. The dose is very low (about 0.4 mSv, like 1–2 months of natural background radiation), and "the benefits ... outweigh any possible harm" (ACS).

**Myth: Mammograms are extremely painful, and squeezing spreads cancer.**
Verdict: PARTLY TRUE for discomfort; MYTH for spreading. Compression can cause "some discomfort" briefly (ACS); tell the technologist if it hurts. It does not cause or spread cancer (NBCF).

**Myth: A biopsy or surgery spreads cancer, or air makes it grow.**
Verdict: MYTH. The risk is "extremely low," surgeons take precautions, and air does not make tumours grow or spread (NCI). Delaying tests out of fear is the bigger worry.

**Myth: Self-exam alone is enough.**
Verdict: MYTH. Self-checks help you know your normal but are not enough for screening (ACS). In the UAE, also have a mammogram every 2 years from 40.

**Myth: My mammogram was normal, so I can ignore a new lump.**
Verdict: MYTH. Screening mammograms miss about 1 in 8 breast cancers (ACS). See a doctor about any new change.

**Myth: Breast cancer is always a death sentence.**
Verdict: MYTH. In the US, 5-year relative survival is over 99% when found early and 92% overall (ACS). Survival falls when it has spread, which is why early detection matters.

**Myth: Herbal remedies can cure breast cancer.**
Verdict: MYTH. No herbal product has been shown to cure cancer, and some interfere with treatment (NCI). Tell your doctor about any herbs, and never delay medical care for them.

## Gulf and UAE beliefs and fears (answer gently)
UAE studies show these concerns are common and understandable.
- **Fear of finding something** was the top screening barrier in Abu Dhabi (Sabih 2012). Most changes are not cancer, and when cancer is found early it is much easier to treat. Checking brings peace of mind or an early start.
- **Shyness about breast exams and preferring a female doctor** is very common and okay (Sabih 2012). You can ask whether a female doctor or technologist is available when you book. Try not to let waiting become a long delay.
- **Herbs, cautery (kay) or healers first:** some UAE women said they would try these first, which delayed treatment (Sabih 2012). No herbal or traditional remedy has been shown to cure cancer (NCI). See a doctor first, and tell them about anything else you use.
- **Fate ("it is God's will"):** fatalistic beliefs exist but were "less widespread than previously assumed" (Sabih 2012). Many families see faith and seeking care as going together; getting checked is a way of caring for your health.
- **"Cancer means death":** in an Ajman student survey, 44.4% believed it is always fatal and 49.7% that little can be done to prevent cancer (Al-Sharbatti 2014). Early-found breast cancer has very high survival.
- **Losing a breast or femininity** (a worry for 62.5% of Ajman students): not all surgery removes the whole breast. A lumpectomy removes only the lump and some tissue around it, and reconstruction can rebuild the breast shape. Options depend on each person; ask the medical team.
- **What others will think, a husband's support, daughters' marriage prospects** (Sabih 2012): breast cancer is not contagious and not caused by anything a person did wrong. Counselling and support programmes can help (see help file).
- **Curse or evil eye** (believed by 19.4% of Ajman students): known risk factors are age, genes, hormones and lifestyle (WHO).
- **Cost:** free screening campaigns run in the UAE, such as Pink Caravan by Friends of Cancer Patients (see help file).
