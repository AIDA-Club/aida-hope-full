# Myth or fact?

Each answer is based on the WHO breast cancer fact sheet (checked 9 October
2026), unless it says otherwise.

**Myth: If nobody in my family had breast cancer, I can't get it.**
Fact: Most women diagnosed with breast cancer have no known family history.
Having no family history does not mean a woman is at low risk.

**Myth: Men can't get breast cancer.**
Fact: Men can get it, although it is rare: about 0.5–1% of breast cancers occur
in men.

**Myth: Every breast lump is cancer.**
Fact: Most breast lumps are not cancer. But any unusual lump should be checked
by a doctor.

**Myth: A lump that doesn't hurt is nothing to worry about.**
Fact: A breast cancer lump is often painless. Anyone with an unusual lump should
see a doctor, even if it doesn't hurt.

**Myth: Breast cancer only happens to people with an unhealthy lifestyle.**
Fact: About 80% of breast cancers develop in women with no modifiable risk
factor other than being female and being over 40.

**Myth: Breast cancer is rare.**
Fact: In 2024, about 2.4 million women worldwide were diagnosed with breast
cancer, and about 694,000 died from it.

**Myth: Finding it early doesn't change anything.**
Fact: Cancers found early, while small and not spread, are more likely to be
treated successfully.

**Myth: Checking only matters once you have symptoms.**
Fact: Early detection has two parts: knowing the signs and seeing a doctor
promptly (early diagnosis), and screening, which looks for cancer in healthy
women before any symptoms appear.

**Myth: Breast ultrasound uses radiation.**
Fact (general knowledge): Ultrasound uses sound waves, not radiation.

**Myth: The booth's AI can tell whether I have cancer.**
Fact: Our AI is an educational demonstration, not a medical device. In testing
on a small set of 68 images it missed 3 of 24 cancers. Only doctors can
diagnose, and a biopsy is how they confirm whether a lump is benign or
malignant.
