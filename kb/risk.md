# Risk factors

Source: WHO breast cancer fact sheet (checked 9 October 2026).

Things that can raise the risk of breast cancer include:

- Getting older.
- Obesity.
- Harmful use of alcohol.
- A family history of breast cancer.
- Exposure to radiation.
- Reproductive history.
- Tobacco use.
- Hormone therapy after menopause (postmenopausal hormone therapy).

Important points:

- About 80% of breast cancers develop in women who have no modifiable risk
  factor other than being female and being over 40. In other words, most women
  who get breast cancer did nothing "wrong".
- Most women diagnosed with breast cancer do not have a known family history.
  Having no family history does not mean a woman is at low risk.
- About 99% of breast cancers occur in women; about 0.5–1% occur in men.

A risk factor is not a diagnosis. Only a healthcare professional can talk
about a person's own risk.
