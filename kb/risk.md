# Breast cancer risk factors and risk reduction

## Key message
- The biggest risk factors are **being female** and **getting older**. About 80% of breast cancers occur in women with no risk factor other than sex and age (WHO).
- Having a risk factor does not mean you will get breast cancer; having none does not mean you cannot.
- Lowering risk helps but is no guarantee, so knowing your normal and screening on time still matter.

## Risk factors you cannot change
- **Sex:** about 99% of breast cancers occur in women, 0.5–1% in men (WHO).
- **Age:** most US cases are in women 55 and older (ACS). Chance of breast cancer in the next 10 years (NCI, 2020 figures): age 30 about 1 in 204; 40 about 1 in 65; 50 about 1 in 42; 60 about 1 in 28; 70 about 1 in 24.
- **Inherited genes:** BRCA1, BRCA2 and PALB2 are the main high-risk genes (WHO); CHEK2, BARD1, RAD51C and RAD51D also raise risk (ACS). Up to 10% of breast cancers are thought to be hereditary (ACS); BRCA1/2 alone explain about 2% (CRUK). A BRCA change gives "up to a 70 in 100" lifetime chance (ACS) or 45–65% by age 70 (CRUK). That is high, but not certain.
- **Family history:** one affected mother, sister or daughter roughly doubles risk; two can triple it. A male relative with breast cancer also raises risk (ACS).
- **Personal history:** previous breast cancer, or some biopsy findings such as LCIS or atypical hyperplasia.
- **Dense breasts** (seen on a mammogram, cannot be felt): up to 3–5 times higher risk in the most dense versus least dense (CRUK).
- **Chest radiation**, especially before age 30 (MoHAP, EHS).
- **Reproductive history:** age periods started and age at first pregnancy (WHO).
- **Height:** taller women have higher risk; the reason is unclear (ACS).

## Risk factors you can influence
- **Excess weight after menopause** (ACS).
- **Alcohol:** "No amount of alcohol is completely safe" (ACS).
- **Inactivity:** the most active women have 13% lower risk than the least active (CRUK).
- **Tobacco:** 7–13% higher risk in current smokers (CRUK); WHO lists tobacco use.
- **Hormones:** menopausal hormone therapy raises risk more with long-term use, certain types, higher doses and starting after 60 (ACS). Hormonal birth control brings a small rise during use that fades after stopping (ACS, NCI). MoHAP: "Abusing alcohol, tobacco and hormonal medications increases the chances of developing breast cancer." Never start or stop medicine without a doctor.
- **Pregnancy and breastfeeding:** never being pregnant or a first pregnancy at 35+ slightly raises risk (ACS); not breastfeeding raises it too.
- UK estimate: 23% of breast cancers are preventable, mainly through weight (8%), alcohol (8%) and breastfeeding (5%) (CRUK). These are UK, not UAE, figures.

## Men
- About 1 in 100 breast cancers (CDC); US lifetime risk about 1 in 755 (ACS 2026).
- Risk factors (CDC): age (mostly after 50); BRCA1/2; a close relative with breast cancer; chest radiation; estrogen-containing drugs; Klinefelter syndrome; liver cirrhosis; obesity in older men; some testicle conditions.

## Young women
- Uncommon but possible: about 10% of US cases are under 45 (CDC); 2.0% at ages 20–34 and 8.6% at 35–44 (SEER 2019–2023).
- In young women it is more likely to be hereditary and more often found later (CDC).
- **UAE:** diagnosis is about 10–15 years younger than in North America and Europe; average age 48 in an Al Ain cohort diagnosed 2008–2012 (PLOS ONE 2021). An EHS radiologist reported cases in women in their twenties (press, Nov 2025).

## Ways to lower risk
- Be active: 150–300 minutes of moderate or 75–150 minutes of vigorous activity a week (ACS).
- Keep a healthy weight and eat a healthy diet (CRUK).
- Avoid alcohol and all tobacco.
- Breastfeed if you can: risk is about 4.3% lower per 12 months of breastfeeding (Lancet 2002). Not everyone can, and that is no one's fault.
- Discuss hormone therapy with a doctor.
- Strong family history or known gene change: ask a doctor about genetic counselling. Specialists can discuss risk-reducing options (WHO). The chatbot gives no treatment advice.

## Key statistics (always give the year)
- **Global, 2024 (WHO citing IARC):** about 2.4 million women diagnosed and 694,000 deaths. Most common cancer in women in 164 of 186 countries.
- **UAE, GLOBOCAN 2024 (IARC estimate):** No. 1 cancer overall and in women. 1,774 new cases (39.1% of female cancers, 20.1% of all cancers) and 482 deaths (12.6% of cancer deaths).
- **UAE registry, 2023 data (press-reported, Nov 2025):** 1,456 new cases; 94 per 100,000 women; 66% found at early stages.
- **MoHAP (Oct 2026):** breast cancer causes 11.6% of cancer deaths (year not stated). "Early diagnosis increases the chances of recovery in 95% of cases."
- GLOBOCAN numbers are modelled estimates and registry numbers are recorded cases; do not compare them as a trend.
- **US comparison (ACS 2026):** lifetime risk about 1 in 8; median age at diagnosis 63. 5-year relative survival (diagnosed 2015–2021): localized over 99%, regional 87%, distant 32%, all stages 92%.
