## know_signs
A new lump, a change in breast shape, skin dimpling, or an unusual nipple change or discharge deserves attention. Many breast changes are not cancer. Ask a healthcare professional about a new or persistent change.

Early breast cancer can have no symptoms. Talk to a healthcare professional about appropriate screening.

[Learn more: National Cancer Institute](https://www.cancer.gov/types/breast/symptoms)

## myth_fact
**Myth: Every breast lump is cancer.**

**Fact:** Many breast changes are not cancer, but a new or persistent change should be checked by a healthcare professional. An AI demonstration cannot determine the cause.

[Learn more: National Cancer Institute](https://www.cancer.gov/types/breast/symptoms)

## explain_term
**Benign:** Not cancerous.

**Malignant:** Cancerous.

**Screening:** Looking for cancer before symptoms appear. Screening results may need follow-up tests; they do not alone establish a diagnosis.

[Screening: National Cancer Institute](https://www.cancer.gov/types/breast/screening) · [Diagnosis: National Cancer Institute](https://www.cancer.gov/types/breast/diagnosis)

## get_help
For a new or persistent breast change, contact a licensed healthcare professional or your local clinic. Ask about assessment and screening appropriate for you.

Booth staff can help you find local services. Please do not enter personal medical information into this demonstration.

[How breast changes are assessed: National Cancer Institute](https://www.cancer.gov/types/breast/diagnosis)
