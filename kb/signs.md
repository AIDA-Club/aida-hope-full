# Warning signs of breast cancer

## Key message
- Look for anything **new or different for you** in the breast, chest or armpit.
- "Most breast lumps are not cancer" (WHO), but every new change should be checked by a doctor.
- A lump that does not hurt is not a reason to relax. WHO says lumps are "often without pain."
- The chatbot cannot diagnose. Only a doctor can tell what a change is.

## Common warning signs (women)
- A new lump, swelling or thickening in the breast, chest or armpit.
- A change in the size or shape of one or both breasts, or swelling of part of a breast even without a lump.
- Skin changes: dimpling, puckering, redness or other colour change, pitting, or skin that looks like orange peel.
- Nipple or breast skin that is red, dry, flaking or thickened, or a rash on the nipple (it may look like eczema).
- A nipple that turns inward (retraction) or changes shape or look.
- Fluid from the nipple when not pregnant or breastfeeding, especially if it contains blood.
- Swollen lymph nodes (lumps) under the arm or near the collarbone. These can appear before a breast lump is big enough to feel (ACS).

**Feel (ACS):** a painless, hard, fixed lump with irregular edges is more likely to be cancer, but cancers can also be soft, round or tender.

## A less-known type: inflammatory breast cancer (IBC)
- Rare: 1–5% of breast cancers in the US. Often diagnosed at a younger age (NCI).
- Usually **no lump**. Symptoms "generally come on rapidly."
- Signs: breast skin that looks pink, reddish-purple or bruised; dimpling or ridges like orange peel; a quick increase in breast size; heaviness, burning or tenderness; an inward-turning nipple; swollen nodes under the arm or near the collarbone.
- It can look like a breast infection (mastitis), so a doctor must check it.

## Signs in men (CDC, 2026)
- A lump or swelling in the breast.
- Redness or flaky skin in the breast.
- Irritation or dimpling of breast skin.
- Nipple discharge.
- Pulling in of the nipple, or pain in the nipple area.
- CDC advice for men with any of these: "see your doctor right away."

## Usually NOT a sign of cancer (but still worth telling a doctor)
- **Breast pain on its own.** NHS: "Breast pain by itself is not usually a symptom of breast cancer." Pain can come from benign conditions (ACS). Pain that is new, does not go away, or comes with other changes should be checked.
- **Lumpiness, tenderness or swelling that changes with the period.** This is often fibrocystic change, which is common and benign.
- **Cysts.** Fluid-filled sacs that are usually not cancer.
- **Fibroadenoma.** A common benign lump in younger women, usually firm, smooth and movable. Only a doctor can confirm it.
- **A lump after a bump or injury.** Injuries do not cause breast cancer. They can cause harmless lumps or make you notice a lump that was already there. If it does not go away, get it checked.

## What to do if you notice a change
1. **Do not wait** for the next screening or for the change to go away. Book a doctor's visit soon.
2. **A recent normal mammogram does not rule it out.** Screening mammograms miss about 1 in 8 breast cancers (ACS).
3. **Stay calm.** Most changes are not cancer, but it's important to get checked (NHS).
4. **Early matters.** NHS: "finding it early may mean it's easier to treat." In the US, 5-year relative survival is over 99% when breast cancer is found before it spreads (ACS).
5. **What the doctor may do:** examine you, then arrange imaging (usually ultrasound first under age 30; mammogram and/or ultrasound from 30) and, if needed, a biopsy. A biopsy is the only way to confirm cancer.
6. **Where:** your family doctor or, for Ajman University students and staff, the University Health Clinic (see the help file).
7. In an emergency, call **998** (ambulance).
