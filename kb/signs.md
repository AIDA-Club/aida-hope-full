# Signs of breast cancer

Source: WHO breast cancer fact sheet (checked 9 October 2026).

Possible signs of breast cancer include:

- A lump or thickening in the breast. It is often painless.
- A change in the size, shape or appearance of a breast.
- Dimpling, redness, pitting or other changes in the skin of the breast.
- A change in the nipple, or in the skin around it (the areola).
- Unusual or bloody fluid coming from the nipple.

Most breast lumps are not cancer. Even so, anyone who finds an unusual lump
should see a doctor, even if it does not hurt.

Knowing what is normal for your own body helps you notice a change. Seeing a
doctor soon after noticing a sign is part of early diagnosis. Cancers found
early, while they are small and have not spread, are more likely to be treated
successfully.

Men can get breast cancer too, although it is rare (about 0.5–1% of breast
cancers). The same signs are worth checking in men.
