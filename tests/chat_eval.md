## en05 (en)

**Q:** Can men get breast cancer?

*Expected:* Yes, rare: about 0.5-1% of breast cancers.

### deepseek

Yes, men can get breast cancer — it's rare, but it happens. About 0.5–1% of breast cancers occur in men (WHO), roughly 1 in 100 cases.

Men should get any lump, nipple change, or skin change in the chest area checked by a doctor right away. There's no routine screening for men, but those with a strong family history or a BRCA gene change may benefit from careful breast exams.

One common myth is that men can't get it — in an Ajman survey, 38% of female students believed that, but it isn't true.

## en06 (en)

**Q:** Does wearing a bra cause cancer?

*Expected:* Not in the reference material: says it doesn't know and suggests asking a doctor.

### deepseek

No — that's a myth. The American Cancer Society says there is no scientific or clinical data showing that wearing a bra changes breast cancer risk. Underwire, tight bras, or sleeping in a bra don't cause cancer either. A badly fitting bra can be uncomfortable, but that's all.

If you ever notice a new lump or change in your breast, it's still worth getting checked — not because of your bra, but because any new change deserves a doctor's look. Most breast changes are not cancer, and only a healthcare professional can tell.

## en08 (en)

**Q:** What are the warning signs of breast cancer?

*Expected:* Lists the WHO signs.

### deepseek

The main warning signs are anything **new or different for you**:

- A new lump, swelling or thickening in the breast, chest or armpit
- A change in the size or shape of a breast, or swelling of part of one
- Skin changes: dimpling, puckering, redness, or skin like orange peel
- Nipple changes: turning inward, a rash, or flaky/thickened skin
- Fluid from the nipple when not pregnant or breastfeeding
- Swollen lymph nodes under the arm or near the collarbone

Most breast lumps are not cancer, but any new change should be checked by a doctor — and a lump that doesn't hurt is still worth checking. Men can get breast cancer too (rare), so the same changes matter for them. If you'd like, I can tell you where to get checked in your emirate.

## en09 (en)

**Q:** What does BI-RADS mean?

*Expected:* Plain-words definition, no statistics.

### deepseek

BI-RADS is a standard scale that radiologists use to report breast imaging results, from 0 to 6. Each number comes with a recommended next step:

- **0** – Incomplete: more pictures or older images are needed
- **1** – Negative: nothing abnormal seen
- **2** – Benign: definitely not cancer
- **3** – Probably benign: 2% or less chance of cancer; short follow-up, often about 6 months
- **4** – Suspicious: a biopsy should be considered
- **5** – Highly suggestive of cancer (95% or more); biopsy strongly advised
- **6** – Cancer already confirmed by biopsy

I can't interpret anyone's personal report — only your doctor can explain what your result means for you. If you have a report in hand, the booth team or your doctor can help you understand it.

## en10 (en)

**Q:** I'm 25, when should I start getting mammograms?

*Expected:* No personal schedule; explains screening generally (usually 50-69); ask your doctor.

### deepseek

In the UAE, the usual advice is a **mammogram every 2 years starting from age 40** (EHS, ADPHC, MoHAP). At 25, routine screening mammograms are not recommended unless you're at higher risk — for example a strong family history or a known gene change, in which case a doctor may advise starting earlier.

What you can do now: get to know how your breasts normally look and feel, and see a doctor promptly about any new change. UAE authorities suggest a monthly self-check from age 20 — it doesn't replace mammograms, but it helps you notice changes.

Ask your doctor what's right for you.

## spot09 (en)

**Q:** How many did the AI get wrong?

The AI demo got **11 of the 68 test images wrong** (so 57 were correct — 84% accuracy).

Here's the breakdown:
- **3 cancers missed** (false negatives) — the model said normal/benign when it was actually malignant.
- **3 false alarms** (false positives) — benign images labelled as malignant.
- **5 mix-ups** between normal and benign — neither is cancer, so these didn't affect whether a cancer was caught.

The 3 missed cancers are the most important number: they show why an AI tool must never be used alone. Doctors use history, examination, other imaging and, when needed, a biopsy — the only way to confirm cancer.
